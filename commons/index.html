How it works

  1. Four teams share one lake of fish.
  2. Each round, every team secretly writes down how many fish it wants to catch (0 to the max). Collect the slips and type the numbers in.
  3. Press Reveal. Each team gets what it asked for. If the lake can't cover every request, the catch is split in proportion to what each team asked for.
  4. Press Regrow. Whatever is left in the lake multiplies, up to its capacity.
  5. If the lake hits zero it collapses and the game ends early. If that happens before the last round, no one wins.
  6. Score = total fish caught by your team, minus any fines.

Instructor tips: keep the round count hidden to avoid "last round" grabs. Run a few rounds silent, then tick Teams may talk this round and see whether communication changes behavior.

Anonymous mode hides each team's catch so nobody can tell who overfished (no monitoring). It can be switched on or off during the game, and the results screen always shows the full breakdown.

No-winner rule: turn it off for a pure Hardin game, where teams keep their catch even if the lake collapses. Turn it on when you want the class to share a stake in the lake lasting.

Fines (under the scoreboard) subtract points from a team's score, for sanctions the class agrees on. Fines don't change the lake.

Keyboard: Enter moves between team boxes and reveals; Enter again advances.

Progress is saved in this browser, so an accidental refresh won't lose the game.

    Discussion

    • What was each team's strategy? Did it change as the lake shrank?
    • Why was it rational for a single team to take more, even when it hurt everyone?
    • Did being allowed to talk change anything? Did any team break a promise?
    • Compare games: which changes (talking, rules, fines, monitoring) made the biggest difference?
    • What rules (quotas, property rights, monitoring, penalties) could have prevented overuse?
    • Where do we see this in the real world? Fisheries, groundwater, carbon emissions, antibiotics...
